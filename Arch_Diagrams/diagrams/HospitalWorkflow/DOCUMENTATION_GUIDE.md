# Patient Admission Documentation Guide
## Clinical Information Flow and Documentation Requirements

### Overview
This guide provides comprehensive documentation requirements for patient admission following IHI quality improvement principles. It emphasizes documentation as a patient safety tool, care coordination mechanism, and regulatory compliance requirement.

---

## Purpose of Clinical Documentation

### Primary Purposes
1. **Patient Safety** - Accurate information prevents errors
2. **Care Coordination** - Communication across providers
3. **Regulatory Compliance** - Joint Commission, CMS requirements
4. **Quality Measurement** - Track outcomes and improvement
5. **Legal Protection** - Defend care decisions
6. **Reimbursement** - Justify medical necessity

### Documentation as Quality Tool
- Supports evidence-based care
- Enables performance measurement
- Facilitates quality improvement
- Ensures continuity of care
- Protects patient rights

---

## Required Documentation Elements

### 1. Patient Demographics & Registration
**Responsible**: Registration/Admitting
**Timing**: Upon arrival
**Required Elements**:
- Full legal name
- Date of birth
- Medical record number (MRN)
- Address and contact information
- Insurance information and authorization
- Emergency contacts (minimum 2)
- Primary care physician
- Preferred language
- Religious/cultural preferences

**Quality Check**: Verify two patient identifiers

---

### 2. Consents & Legal Documents
**Responsible**: Registration/Nursing
**Timing**: Before treatment begins
**Required Elements**:

- **Informed Consent for Treatment**
  - Explanation of proposed treatment
  - Risks and benefits discussed
  - Alternatives explained
  - Patient questions answered
  - Signature obtained

- **HIPAA Privacy Notice**
  - Privacy practices explained
  - Patient rights reviewed
  - Acknowledgment signed

- **Patient Rights and Responsibilities**
  - Rights to refuse treatment
  - Right to privacy
  - Right to information
  - Responsibilities for care

- **Advance Directives** (if applicable)
  - Living will
  - Healthcare power of attorney
  - DNR/DNI orders
  - POLST/MOLST forms

- **Financial Responsibility Agreement**
  - Insurance coverage explained
  - Patient financial responsibility
  - Payment arrangements

**Quality Check**: All signatures, dates, and witnesses documented

---

### 3. Nursing Triage & Assessment
**Responsible**: ED Nurse
**Timing**: Within 15 minutes of arrival
**Required Elements**:

**Chief Complaint**:
- In patient's own words
- Onset and duration
- Associated symptoms
- What brought patient to ED today

**Vital Signs**:
- Blood pressure (BP)
- Heart rate (HR)
- Respiratory rate (RR)
- Temperature (Temp)
- Oxygen saturation (O2 sat)
- Pain level (0-10 scale)
- Weight (if applicable)

**Acuity Level** (ESI 1-5):
- Level 1: Immediate life-threatening
- Level 2: High risk, severe pain
- Level 3: Stable, multiple resources
- Level 4: Stable, one resource
- Level 5: Minor, no resources

**Allergies** (CRITICAL):
- Medication allergies
- Food allergies
- Environmental allergies
- Type of reaction
- Severity of reaction

**Safety Assessments**:
- Fall risk score (Morse Fall Scale)
- Skin integrity (Braden Scale)
- Pressure injury risk
- Suicide risk screening
- Abuse/neglect screening
- Substance use screening
- Isolation precautions needed

**Quality Check**: All allergies documented in multiple locations

---

### 4. Medication Reconciliation
**Responsible**: ED Nurse/Pharmacist
**Timing**: Within 24 hours of admission
**Required Elements**:

⚠️ **HIGH RISK AREA** - Medication errors commonly occur here

**Home Medications**:
- Complete list of all medications
- Prescription medications
- Over-the-counter medications
- Herbal supplements
- Vitamins

**For Each Medication Document**:
- Medication name (generic and brand)
- Dosage and strength
- Route of administration
- Frequency
- Last dose taken (date and time)
- Prescribing physician
- Reason for taking

**Adherence Assessment**:
- Does patient take as prescribed?
- Any missed doses?
- Any barriers to adherence?
- Financial concerns?
- Understanding of medications?

**Reconciliation Process**:
1. Obtain list from patient/family
2. Verify with pharmacy records
3. Compare with previous admissions
4. Resolve discrepancies
5. Document in EHR
6. Communicate to admitting physician

**Quality Check**: Pharmacist verification required

---

### 5. Physician History & Physical (H&P)
**Responsible**: ED Physician
**Timing**: Upon assessment
**Required Elements**:

**History of Present Illness (HPI)**:
- Onset of symptoms
- Location
- Duration
- Character/quality
- Aggravating factors
- Relieving factors
- Timing
- Severity

**Past Medical History (PMH)**:
- Chronic conditions
- Previous hospitalizations
- Surgeries
- Significant illnesses

**Past Surgical History (PSH)**:
- All previous surgeries
- Dates and complications
- Anesthesia issues

**Family History**:
- Hereditary conditions
- Age and health of parents/siblings
- Causes of death in family

**Social History**:
- Smoking status (pack-years)
- Alcohol use (drinks per week)
- Illicit drug use
- Occupation
- Living situation
- Support system

**Review of Systems (ROS)**:
- Constitutional
- Eyes, Ears, Nose, Throat
- Cardiovascular
- Respiratory
- Gastrointestinal
- Genitourinary
- Musculoskeletal
- Skin
- Neurological
- Psychiatric
- Endocrine
- Hematologic/Lymphatic
- Allergic/Immunologic

**Physical Examination**:
- General appearance
- Vital signs
- System-by-system examination
- Pertinent positive findings
- Pertinent negative findings

**Assessment**:
- Differential diagnosis
- Working diagnosis
- Severity assessment

**Plan**:
- Diagnostic tests ordered
- Treatment initiated
- Consultations requested
- Disposition (admit vs discharge)

**Quality Check**: H&P completed and signed

---

### 6. Admission Orders
**Responsible**: Hospitalist
**Timing**: Upon acceptance for admission
**Required Elements**:

**Admitting Diagnosis**:
- Primary diagnosis
- Secondary diagnoses
- Comorbidities

**Condition Level**:
- Stable
- Fair
- Serious
- Critical

**Code Status** (CRITICAL):
- Full code (CPR, intubation, all measures)
- DNR (Do Not Resuscitate)
- DNI (Do Not Intubate)
- Limited code (specify limitations)

**Diet Orders**:
- Regular
- Cardiac
- Diabetic
- Renal
- NPO (nothing by mouth)
- Texture modifications
- Fluid restrictions

**Activity Level**:
- Bed rest
- Bed rest with bathroom privileges
- Up in chair
- Ambulate with assistance
- Ambulate independently
- Physical therapy consult

**Vital Sign Frequency**:
- Every 15 minutes (critical)
- Every 1 hour (unstable)
- Every 4 hours (stable)
- Every shift (routine)

**Nursing Care Orders**:
- Intake and output monitoring
- Daily weights
- Wound care
- Catheter care
- IV site care
- Fall precautions
- Isolation precautions

**Quality Check**: All orders clear, complete, and signed

---

### 7. Medication Orders
**Responsible**: Hospitalist
**Timing**: Upon admission
**Required Elements**:

**Continue Home Medications**:
- Review medication reconciliation
- Continue appropriate medications
- Hold medications as needed
- Adjust doses if required

**New Medications**:
- Indication documented
- Dose, route, frequency
- Duration of therapy
- Special instructions

**PRN (As Needed) Medications**:
- Indication for use
- Maximum dose/frequency
- Parameters for administration

**IV Fluids**:
- Type of fluid
- Rate of infusion
- Duration
- Monitoring parameters

**Quality Check**: Pharmacist review for interactions and allergies

---

### 8. Nursing Admission Assessment
**Responsible**: Floor Nurse
**Timing**: Within 1 hour of arrival to floor
**Required Elements**:

**Head-to-Toe Physical Assessment**:

**Neurological**:
- Level of consciousness (Alert, Oriented x4)
- Pupil size and reactivity
- Motor strength
- Sensory function
- Speech

**Cardiovascular**:
- Heart rate and rhythm
- Peripheral pulses
- Capillary refill
- Edema
- Chest pain

**Respiratory**:
- Respiratory rate and pattern
- Breath sounds
- Oxygen saturation
- Cough
- Sputum

**Gastrointestinal**:
- Bowel sounds
- Abdomen (soft, tender, distended)
- Last bowel movement
- Nausea/vomiting
- Appetite

**Genitourinary**:
- Urinary output
- Color and clarity
- Continence
- Catheter (if present)

**Musculoskeletal**:
- Range of motion
- Strength
- Gait
- Mobility aids

**Skin**:
- Color, temperature, moisture
- Turgor
- Wounds or lesions
- Pressure injuries
- IV sites

**Pain Assessment**:
- Location
- Quality (sharp, dull, burning)
- Severity (0-10 scale)
- Duration
- Aggravating/relieving factors

**Psychosocial**:
- Mood and affect
- Anxiety level
- Coping mechanisms
- Support system
- Cultural/spiritual needs

**Quality Check**: Complete assessment documented within 1 hour

---

### 9. Nursing Care Plan
**Responsible**: Floor Nurse
**Timing**: Within 8 hours of admission
**Required Elements**:

**Nursing Diagnoses** (NANDA format):
- Actual diagnoses (currently present)
- Risk diagnoses (potential problems)
- Health promotion diagnoses

**Patient Goals**:
- Specific and measurable
- Achievable
- Relevant to patient
- Time-bound
- Patient-centered

**Nursing Interventions**:
- Evidence-based actions
- Frequency specified
- Rationale documented

**Expected Outcomes**:
- Measurable criteria
- Timeline for achievement
- Evaluation method

**Evaluation**:
- Progress toward goals
- Effectiveness of interventions
- Need for plan revision

**Quality Check**: Care plan individualized to patient

---

### 10. Case Management Documentation
**Responsible**: Case Manager
**Timing**: Within 24-48 hours of admission
**Required Elements**:

**Utilization Review**:
- Medical necessity justification
- Appropriate level of care
- Insurance authorization obtained
- Estimated length of stay
- Barriers to discharge

**Discharge Planning**:
- Anticipated discharge needs
- Home health services
- Durable medical equipment (DME)
- Skilled nursing facility
- Rehabilitation services
- Follow-up appointments

**Social Assessment**:
- Living situation
- Support system
- Financial resources
- Transportation
- Health literacy
- Social determinants of health

**Coordination**:
- Insurance updates
- Service referrals
- Equipment orders
- Appointment scheduling
- Community resources

**Quality Check**: Discharge planning initiated early

## Critical Handoff Documentation

### Handoff #1: ED Nurse → ED Physician
**Risk**: Incomplete triage information delays diagnosis
**Mitigation**: Standardized triage template

**Required Documentation**:
- Complete vital signs
- Chief complaint (patient's words)
- Pertinent history
- Allergies
- Current medications
- Recent interventions

**Format**: Face-to-face communication + EHR documentation

---

### Handoff #2: ED Physician → Hospitalist
**Risk**: Incomplete clinical picture leads to errors
**Mitigation**: SBAR format, complete EHR review

**Required Documentation**:
- Clinical findings
- Diagnostic test results
- Treatment provided in ED
- Response to treatment
- Reason for admission
- Pending issues

**Format**: Phone call + EHR documentation

---

### Handoff #3: ED Nurse → Floor Nurse
**Risk**: Information loss during transfer
**Mitigation**: Structured handoff tool, bedside report

**Required Documentation** (SBAR):
- **S**ituation: Current condition, reason for admission
- **B**ackground: Medical history, medications, allergies
- **A**ssessment: Current vital signs, recent changes
- **R**ecommendation: Pending orders, concerns, family issues

**Format**: Bedside handoff + written transfer note

## Documentation Quality Metrics

### Timeliness Standards
- H&P completed: Within 24 hours of admission
- Medication reconciliation: Within 24 hours
- Nursing admission assessment: Within 1 hour
- Nursing care plan: Within 8 hours
- Progress notes: Daily minimum
- Discharge summary: Within 30 days

### Completeness Standards
- All required elements present
- No blank fields in critical areas
- Signatures and credentials on all entries
- Date and time stamps accurate
- Addendums properly documented

### Accuracy Standards
- Information consistent across documents
- Allergies documented in all locations
- Medication list matches all sources
- Vital signs match nursing documentation
- Diagnoses consistent throughout record

### Legibility Standards
- Electronic documentation preferred
- Handwritten notes legible
- No unapproved abbreviations
- Standard medical terminology
- Proper grammar and spelling

## Regulatory Requirements

### Joint Commission Standards
- **National Patient Safety Goals**:
  - Two patient identifiers
  - Medication reconciliation at transitions
  - Fall risk assessment
  - Pressure injury prevention
  - Hand hygiene compliance

- **Documentation Requirements**:
  - H&P within 24 hours
  - Operative reports immediately
  - Discharge summary timely
  - Informed consent documented

### CMS (Medicare/Medicaid) Requirements
- **Medical Necessity**: Documentation supports admission
- **Two-Midnight Rule**: Inpatient vs observation status
- **Physician Certification**: Admission appropriateness
- **Discharge Planning**: Documented and timely
- **Quality Measures**: Core measure documentation

### HIPAA Privacy Requirements
- **Patient Consent**: Treatment consent obtained
- **Privacy Notice**: Acknowledgment documented
- **Minimum Necessary**: Only required information shared
- **Secure Practices**: Protected health information secured
- **Breach Notification**: Incidents documented and reported

## Documentation Best Practices

### 1. Document in Real-Time
**Why**: Reduces errors, ensures accuracy, improves efficiency
**How**: 
- Use mobile devices at bedside
- Document immediately after care
- Avoid end-of-shift charting
- Use voice recognition when appropriate

### 2. Use Objective Language
**Why**: Facts are defensible, opinions are not
**How**:
- Describe what you see, hear, smell
- Use measurable terms
- Avoid judgmental language
- Quote patient statements

**Examples**:
- ✓ "Patient states 'I feel dizzy when I stand up'"
- ✗ "Patient is non-compliant"
- ✓ "Wound measures 3cm x 2cm with moderate serous drainage"
- ✗ "Wound looks bad"

### 3. Document Patient Responses
**Why**: Shows effectiveness of care
**How**:
- Response to interventions
- Response to medications
- Response to education
- Changes in condition

**Example**: "Pain level decreased from 8/10 to 4/10 thirty minutes after administration of morphine 4mg IV"

### 4. Document Communication
**Why**: Shows care coordination
**How**:
- Physician notifications
- Family updates
- Interdisciplinary communication
- Consultation requests

**Example**: "Dr. Smith notified at 1430 of patient's increased shortness of breath. New orders received for chest x-ray and increase in oxygen to 4L/min"

### 5. Never Alter Documentation
**Why**: Legal and ethical requirement
**How**:
- Use addendum feature in EHR
- Note date and time of addendum
- Explain reason for addition
- Never delete or modify original entry
